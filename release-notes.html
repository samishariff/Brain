What changed in 1.0.8.0

This update improves call recording and the live transcript. Brain can capture the other side of Microsoft Teams calls, reduces audio loss on busy PCs, and asks before stopping a call after a long quiet stretch. It tells you, even from the tray, when something needs attention. It also brings the recent Brain for Mac improvements to Windows.

Call recording improvements

  • Brain can record the other side of Teams calls. New Teams plays call audio in a way Windows does not let an app capture on its own, so Brain uses the call's output device when available and records everything playing there. Brain tells you this before it records automatically, and Record and the tray show which device it is. If Brain cannot hear the other side, it says so.
  • Less audio lost on busy PCs. Earlier versions could drop about half of a call's audio without saying so. This update fixes that capture bottleneck and reduces processor use. If a recording does lose a second or more, Review marks it for a quality check.
  • Other call apps are protected too. Calls in apps other than Teams and Zoom get the same checks for a silent call track. When you record a browser, Brain says it is recording every tab, and it now recognizes more browsers.
  • More control over when recording stops. After a long quiet stretch, or after four hours, Brain asks whether to keep recording instead of counting down.
  • Recording tells you the truth. Brain says "Recording" only once your devices are open, and tells you if Windows is still waiting for microphone permission.
  • Choose how calls are handled during setup. Setup now asks whether Brain should ask you first or record detected calls automatically. Ask first is selected for you.
  • Answer late without losing the start of a call. When Brain asks whether to record a call, it can now record on this PC while you decide. Record keeps the call from the start; Skip deletes it, with a few seconds to undo. If the call ends before you answer, Brain asks whether to keep the recording and deletes it after 15 minutes. It's on for new installs; if you already used Ask first, turn it on in Settings › Recording.
  • Brain follows mute in the Teams and Zoom desktop apps. When Brain detects mute, it records silence for your microphone and keeps recording the call. A short microphone buffer helps cover the detection delay. Brain keeps the detected mute while the call window is minimised or you share your screen, subject to the timeout described below. A setting turns this off. Brain's own microphone switch is now "Record my voice".
  • Notifications for calls. A notification offers Record or Skip, and repeats once if you miss it. When Brain records automatically, a notification offers Stop and save or Discard. If a call sounds quiet while Brain is in the tray, a notification tells you.
  • Brain's own playback stays out of your recordings. Review, People and voice-sample playback pause while Brain is recording everything on a device.
  • A clearer microphone meter that follows your voice and shows when Brain hears you.

Live transcript

  • Optional live speaker preview. Enable it separately in Settings to try the new English speech tools, supporting up to four voices per microphone or meeting-audio track. It is off by default and uses an additional download of about 875 MB. Compatible processors and at least 6 GiB of free memory at recording start are required. Brain tells you when it falls back to your existing tools. Earlier text and fewer speaker-attributed errors have been measured on short test recordings; performance on your calls can differ. Existing recordings are not reprocessed.
  • Your recording continues if live speech processing pauses. Brain retains the words already shown, reports the pause and processes the recorded audio after you stop.
  • Improved filtering in the existing speech tools. Their live text filters reduce noise-only output, notes like "[Silence]", and repeated phrases.
  • You are told when words are not coming through, and the live status no longer flickers during quiet moments or shows "Paused" for a moment every minute.
  • Easier to read. One speaker's words flow into paragraphs under a speaker label instead of short broken lines, even while Brain is still telling voices apart.
  • Live text settles faster. Finished sentences stop changing sooner, so the text you are reading rewrites itself far less.
  • Scroll back freely. Scrolling up stays where you put it, and a Latest button takes you back to the newest words.
  • Fewer repeated words from speakers. On speakers, your microphone also hears the other side. The live transcript hides matching echoes of the other side, and you can show the hidden lines.
  • Clearer live text. Words that may still change show in a lighter colour and aren't copied, and short replies no longer break up the other person's sentences. Copy includes times and speakers.

Meeting assistant

  • A more responsive assistant during calls. Updates are batched to reduce UI stalls, and speech processing takes priority when resources are limited.
  • Answers during the meeting when your PC has room. On capable graphics cards, private AI stays loaded alongside speech. Otherwise the assistant says so before you ask, keeps your questions, and answers them after the meeting with a notification.

People and speaker names

  • Name speakers in older meetings on your terms. People can suggest names for your five most recent meetings or ones you pick, with a preview first, and you can pause, continue or cancel. Automatic suggestions stay within a small hourly allowance.
  • Brain waits for the right moment. Optional AI work pauses while you record, dictate or process, when memory is low, and when Windows Energy Saver is on.
  • Apply now: Review offers it when an approved report is waiting for speaker names.
  • People opens faster. Voices and names appear first, and the detailed checks follow in the background.
  • Name a speaker during a meeting from a searchable list of people, or create a new person, and get one notice for each naming action.
  • Restore someone you removed by choosing them when you name a speaker during a meeting. Long names in People now wrap at large text sizes.
  • Renaming a person in Directory now asks for the new name.

Everything else

  • Speak waits until your microphone is ready, shows "Getting mic ready…" and gives Bluetooth headsets a moment to settle. If it can't hear you, it says so.
  • Report a problem has its own toolbar button. Your report is saved as a draft, you can attach up to three screenshots from your PC, and nothing is sent until you choose to. brain diagnostics export saves the same private report from the command line.
  • Actions: open an action by double-clicking anywhere on its row. Grouping by meeting stays quick in large libraries. A combined meeting's action no longer shows twice after Trash, and connected assistants now see those actions.
  • Setup fits your PC's memory. If there isn't enough free memory for the standard private AI, setup offers the smaller one and says why. You can still choose the standard one. The microphone check adjusts to room noise, and Narrator reads it.
  • Accessibility: fixes for High Contrast and large text across the shell, Record, Review and Actions.
  • Reliability: transcripts are kept when a re-run finds no speech, a slow helper is far less likely to show a false "restart Brain" message, private folders work for administrator accounts, repeated routine entries no longer push real problems out of the failure history, and many smaller fixes.

Known limits

  • Live text and speaker labels can take time to appear and can change while Brain receives more audio. A provisional speaker label may be wrong. The saved transcript is processed again after the call, but speaker assignments can still need your review.
  • The new English live labels distinguish anonymous voices; they do not automatically identify enrolled people. Short overlapping replies can still be duplicated or assigned to the wrong voice. Saved transcription continues to use the existing tools, so its speaker labels may differ from the live view.
  • A word waits up to five additional seconds for a live label after it first appears. Missing or late labels remain unknown; this is not a guarantee of recognition speed or correct attribution.
  • Recording a Teams call records everything playing on that device, including other apps.
  • If Brain asks first and records while you decide, the recording starts when Brain detects the call (a few seconds in), not before. The live transcript starts when you choose Record; the part before that is in the saved transcript after the call. If you used Ask first before 1.0.8, recording while you decide is off until you turn it on, and nothing is recorded before you answer.
  • When Zoom switches how Brain hears the call, a few seconds before the switch can be missing. Review marks that recording for a quality check.
  • Brain can't see mute in a browser call; use Record my voice when you mute there. If it can't see the mute button in Teams or Zoom for about 2 minutes, it records your microphone again and tells you.
  • Vulkan speech acceleration for AMD and Intel graphics has not been tested on that hardware.
  • The battery and Energy Saver rules for AI work have not been tested on a real laptop.
  • Signing in to Google or Microsoft calendars is not available yet. ICS and webcal calendar links work.

Make a complete backup before updating.

What Brain includes

  • Guided setup for speech, summaries, and questions about your meetings.
  • Conversation recording and audio import, with live and saved transcripts and automatic matching to voices you have already named.
  • Editable summaries, suggested decisions, and next steps you can approve.
  • A meeting assistant for questions during a conversation or when reviewing it later.
  • A searchable meeting library and full backups that include recordings.
  • Light and dark appearance, with a navigation menu for smaller windows.

What’s still being checked

Brain is an early preview for practice and feedback. Physical microphones and live calls have not been checked for this preview. These areas remain unverified:

  • Real microphones and computer audio during calls.
  • Longer meetings, changing audio devices, and putting the PC to sleep.

The speech models in this preview support English. Live transcription and automatic matching to voices you have already named have been measured using test recordings on one desktop PC. Laptop performance has not been measured, and processing may be slower.

Google and Microsoft calendar sign-in is not enabled yet. You can add calendar links or import calendar files in Settings.

Before your first meeting

Start with a short practice conversation. Tell everyone before recording and check that you can hear every audio source afterward.

The first setup needs an internet connection and room for the speech and summary tools. After setup, those tools can work on your PC without sending meeting content to an online AI service.

Transcripts, summaries, and chat answers can miss details. Review anything important before relying on it or sharing it. Keep another copy of recordings and notes you want to retain.

A personal signing certificate can still produce a Windows security warning. The first installation asks an administrator to trust Sami Shariff’s app certificate. The setup guide explains what to expect.

Updates and moving your work

The installed app asks before updating by default. You can also choose installation when you exit after saving. Brain reminds you when a checked update is ready, shows what’s new in it, and lets you skip a version.

Keep a full backup, including recordings, before updating or returning to an earlier version. Version 1.0.8.0 keeps database schema 47 and the existing saved-transcript tools; its optional live preview does not activate the experimental saved-transcript engine. An unchanged schema is not a guarantee that every later edit behaves identically in an older app. Retain the complete matching backup and earlier installer, and ask Sami for help before rolling back. Updating from 1.0.3.19 or earlier changes the saved meeting format; returning to those versions requires restoring the matching earlier database copy.

If you have an earlier portable preview, use the Settings option that imports an earlier preview’s work to bring its work into the installed app. Keep the original folder until you’ve checked the imported meetings and recordings.
