What changed in 1.0.6.0

A large update: Brain now runs on more PCs, sets up faster, and is steadier during long days of meetings.

Runs on more PCs

  • Works on PCs with 8 GB of memory. Brain picks speech and private AI settings that fit your PC, and uses lighter speech models when memory is tight.
  • On PCs with less than 16 GB, Brain can now run a smaller private AI, so summaries and chat work on those PCs too. It is a one-time download of about 3.1 GB and is slightly less precise than the larger model, so check its to-dos before relying on them.
  • When memory is short, Brain waits for it to free up and then carries on by itself, instead of stopping with an error.
  • The setup app is now about 14 MB instead of about 144 MB, and the app package is much smaller. Some speech components are downloaded during setup instead.

Setup and updates

  • Guided setup gets recording and transcription working first. Summaries and chat are an optional later step, and Brain no longer offers a private AI model that is too large for your PC.
  • Setup can end with an optional check: record a short example and Brain confirms it was transcribed.
  • A new Use built-in AI only setting keeps summaries and chat on Brain’s private AI.
  • Brain tells you once when an update is ready, shows what changed before you install it, and offers Remind me tomorrow and Skip this version. Failed update checks now back off.
  • Problem reports include a short record of recent setup steps, with personal details removed.

Recording, meetings and people

  • When a detected call goes quiet, Brain counts down with Keep recording and Stop now before it stops. If a new call in the same app starts within 2 minutes, Brain offers to continue the previous meeting.
  • In Library, you can combine processed meetings from the same group into one meeting, and separate them again.
  • Brain names speakers it recognises from voices you have confirmed before, marked Auto, and you can name voices while a meeting is still recording. Automatic names can be turned off in Settings.
  • Search finds meetings by the names of their speakers, and each approved version keeps the transcript it was accepted from.
  • People review groups unnamed voices that sound alike, keeps your place, and lists unrecognised voices first.
  • Actions gain a History you can restore from, a detail panel, grouping and Undo. Accept in Review explains what is holding it back, and unsaved Review edits are kept when you close Brain.
  • You can change which calendar event a meeting belongs to, or choose none, and undo the change.
  • Library, Today, Review and search stay quick even with 2,000 meetings.
  • Speak checks that dictated text really arrived, and is ready as soon as a meeting ends.

Reliability and security

  • Summaries no longer fail because of an item that can’t be matched to the transcript, invented times are ignored, and AI speaker-name suggestions can no longer keep the AI busy without end.
  • Assistants you connect to Brain can follow a meeting live and help with review, within the access you give them.
  • Model downloads are checked more strictly, backups are readable only by your Windows account, and there are many smaller fixes for speech processing, shutdown and recovery.

Your meetings, recordings and settings are kept when you update. See Updates and moving your work before updating from 1.0.3.19.

What Brain includes

  • Guided setup for speech, summaries, and questions about your meetings.
  • Conversation recording and audio import, with live and saved transcripts and automatic matching to voices you have already named.
  • Editable summaries, suggested decisions, and next steps you can approve.
  • A meeting assistant for questions during a conversation or when reviewing it later.
  • A searchable meeting library and full backups that include recordings.
  • Light and dark appearance, with a navigation menu for smaller windows.

What’s still being checked

Brain is an early preview for practice and feedback. Physical microphones and live calls have not been checked for this preview. These areas remain unverified:

  • Real microphones and computer audio during calls.
  • Longer meetings, changing audio devices, and putting the PC to sleep.

The speech models in this preview support English. Live transcription and automatic matching to voices you have already named have been measured using test recordings on one desktop PC. Laptop performance has not been measured, and processing may be slower.

Google and Microsoft calendar sign-in is not enabled yet. You can add calendar links or import calendar files in Settings.

Before your first meeting

Start with a short practice conversation. Tell everyone before recording and check that you can hear every audio source afterward.

The first setup needs an internet connection and room for the speech and summary tools. After setup, those tools can work on your PC without sending meeting content to an online AI service.

Transcripts, summaries, and chat answers can miss details. Review anything important before relying on it or sharing it. Keep another copy of recordings and notes you want to retain.

A personal signing certificate can still produce a Windows security warning. The first installation asks an administrator to trust Sami Shariff’s app certificate. The setup guide explains what to expect.

Updates and moving your work

The installed app asks before updating by default. You can also choose installation when you exit after saving. From 1.0.6.0, Brain reminds you when a checked update is ready, shows what’s new in it, and lets you skip a version.

Keep a full backup before trying an update. Version 1.0.6.0 changes the saved meeting format, and Brain keeps a copy of your meeting database from before the upgrade. Returning to 1.0.3.19 or earlier requires restoring that copy. If an update fails, keep your existing app and ask Sami for help.

If you have an earlier portable preview, use the Settings option that imports an earlier preview’s work to bring its work into the installed app. Keep the original folder until you’ve checked the imported meetings and recordings.
